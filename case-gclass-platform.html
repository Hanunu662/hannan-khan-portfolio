The problem

After the website redesign, the office was the bottleneck. Bookings, hours and progress sheets were tracked by hand, so students, instructors and the office rarely saw the same thing.

What I built

A Student app, an Instructor app with an office mode, a desktop office portal, and Zun Drivers, an online course for the in-class part of Beginner Driver Education.

Where it is

The portal and the online course are live. Both iOS apps are in TestFlight beta, running on real phones with real lessons.

My role

I lead product and UX across all four: what each screen is for, how the flows work, and the words on every screen.

Zun Drivers, the online course

The in-class part of Beginner Driver Education, online: 20 hours of digital lessons plus 10 hours of Homelinks for new Ontario drivers. Ontario has strict rules for online driver education, so the job was making those rules feel like help instead of hoops.

01
Explain the rules, don't just enforce them

The required steps (your details, a tutorial, the code of conduct and the syllabus) each say why they're there, so a new driver isn't stuck guessing.

02
Plain words first

A searchable glossary covers the 51 terms the course uses, from the 5-hour daily limit to Homelinks.

03
Send people to the right help

Technical, service and course-content questions each go to their own team, with phone, email and hours listed right there.

Decisions that shaped it

01
Keep the paper sheet, just make it digital

The in-car progress sheet instructors already used became the skills screen, with the same categories and the same A to D grades, so nobody had to relearn how to grade a student.

02
One job per screen

Logging a lesson is four short steps, booking is three, and help, payments and passwords each get their own page.

03
The office stays in control

Nobody signs themselves up. The office creates every login, approves lessons and hours before they count, and handles password resets, which keeps student records private.

04
Private by default

Phone notifications never show a student's name. They just say to open the app for the details.
