Apex Elite Security

A 13-page marketing site for a licensed Ontario security guard and patrol company, built to read as credible to the property managers and facility owners who actually hire security vendors.

The challenge

A licensed Ontario security company needed a site that would read as credible to the property managers who actually hire security vendors.

My approach

No formal research phase. I worked from the brief and the audience, leading with the credentials property managers check first and segmenting content by property type.

The result

13 pages shipped and live at apexelitesecurity.ca, handling quotes, contact, and hiring for the business today.

My contribution

Solo project. I designed and built all 13 pages, wrote the copy, set up the forms, and added the structured data markup.

The brief

  • Commercial security buyers evaluate vendors on licensing, insurance, and vetting before anything else, so the site needed to answer those questions before a visitor had to ask.
  • Different audiences (retail, healthcare, education, events, residential/condo) each needed to see themselves reflected quickly, without digging
  • Inquiries needed a direct path to a real reply, without requiring the client to manage a backend or pay for form infrastructure

Structure

  • 13 pages: home, services, industries, about, students program, careers, quote, contact, FAQ, accessibility, privacy, terms, and a custom 404
  • Dedicated Industries section covering retail & commercial, healthcare, educational institutions, events & private functions, and residential/condo
  • A distinct Student & Institution Program page for discounted campus and school coverage

Why I made these calls

There was no research phase on this one. These are the calls I made during the build.

01
Lead with trust signals

The homepage states licensing under Ontario's Private Security and Investigative Services Act, insurance, and guard vetting immediately, since those are the exact things a property manager checks before they even ask.

02
Segment by site type

Rather than one generic services page, coverage is broken out by property type (retail, healthcare, education, events, residential), so each visitor sees language written for their situation.

03
Remove friction from contact

Quote, contact, and careers forms route directly to a working inbox with no backend to manage, so a real inquiry becomes a real conversation instead of getting lost. The forms are built to switch to a hosted form service with a single line of code once the client wants to remove the email-app step entirely.

04
Leave regulatory claims blank rather than guess

The PSISA business licence number and the commercial general liability coverage amount are left as clearly marked placeholders throughout the site rather than invented. A licence number is a regulatory credential and a coverage figure is a legal representation to clients. Property managers verify both, so guessing either would expose the business to real liability.

05
Structured data for local search

Each page carries SecurityService schema markup (service area, contact, credentials) so search engines can surface the business correctly for local, credential-sensitive searches rather than generic web results.

Coverage areas

  • Security guard services, mobile patrol, and event security & escort
  • Concierge, lobby security, and parking enforcement
  • CCTV, access control, and physical security installations

What I worked from

The brief and the audience, not a study.

Insight 01

Trust is the whole pitch

Property managers check licensing, insurance, and vetting before anything else, so those had to lead.

Insight 02

One audience, several contexts

Retail, healthcare, education, events, and residential each read the same service differently.

Insight 03

A guessed credential is a liability

A licence number or coverage figure is a legal representation to clients, not decorative copy.

Exploring the options

The decisions I weighed during the build.

Considered

  • One generic services page versus segmenting by property type
  • Filling in plausible licence and insurance placeholders versus leaving them clearly blank
  • A hosted form service versus routing forms straight to an inbox

Chosen

  • Segmented by site type, so each visitor sees language written for their situation
  • Placeholders left clearly marked rather than invented
  • Forms to a working inbox now, with a one-line upgrade path to a form service later

Designing for everyone

Built for facility managers scanning fast on a phone, not designers.

What was built in

  • Plain language throughout, with regulatory terms explained rather than assumed
  • Structured data (schema.org SecurityService markup) so search engines and assistive tools can interpret the pages correctly
  • Forms that work with no backend, so a real inquiry becomes a real conversation instead of failing silently

What came out of it

All 13 pages shipped and are live at apexelitesecurity.ca. Not a mockup. It's the working site the business uses today for quotes, contact, and hiring.

What I learned

Building a site with no research phase forced a different kind of discipline. Every decision had to be defensible on its own, since there wasn't a study to point back to. The credential placeholders ended up being the clearest lesson: it would have been easy to just fill in a plausible-looking licence number and move on, but a security company's whole pitch is trustworthiness, so guessing wasn't really an option.
